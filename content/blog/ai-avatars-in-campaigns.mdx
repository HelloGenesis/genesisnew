Brands ask for AI avatars because they have seen a demo. The demo is always
the same: a photoreal presenter delivering a script, flawlessly. It is
impressive, and it is usually the wrong use.

## Where they work

**Volume and variation.** The same explainer in eight languages, updated when
the product changes, without rebooking a shoot. This is the real case, and it
is a strong one.

**Content that has no face today.** Plenty of useful content never gets made
because it does not justify a production day. An avatar makes that content
economic.

**Consistency over time.** A presenter who looks identical across two years of
content is genuinely hard to arrange with a human.

## Where they fail

**Anywhere trust is the point.** Testimonials, founder messages, anything
where the audience is asking "do I believe this person". A synthetic face
answers that question badly.

**Comedy and timing.** Watch an avatar attempt a beat. Then don't.

**When the category is already suspicious.** Finance and health audiences read
a synthetic presenter as evasion, whatever the script says.

## The rule we use

If replacing the presenter with a stock actor would also be fine, an avatar is
fine. If the specific human matters, hire the human.
